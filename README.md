
# GDPR Record of Processing Activities (ROPA) — MedCore Health

## Project Overview
This project simulates the creation of a GDPR-compliant Record of 
Processing Activities (ROPA) under Article 30 GDPR for a fictional 
private healthcare clinic operating in Barcelona, Spain.

## Scenario
**Company**: MedCore Health S.L. — a private clinic based in 
Barcelona with 30 employees, processing special category health 
data of patients under Art. 9 GDPR.

## What This Project Covers
- 8 realistic data processing activities mapped to their legal basis
- Dual legal basis documentation (Art. 6 + Art. 9) for health data
- Third-party processor and data sharing agreement register
- DPIA register identifying high-risk processing activities
- Application of Ley 41/2002, Real Decreto 2210/1995 and 
  Ley 31/1995 alongside GDPR
- Distinction between Data Processing Agreements (DPA) and 
  Data Sharing Agreements (DSA)

## Key Difference vs Fintech ROPA
Unlike financial data, health data is special category data 
under Art. 9 GDPR. Every processing activity involving clinical 
records requires a dual legal basis:
1. Art. 6 — General lawfulness
2. Art. 9 — Special category legitimacy

## Skills Demonstrated
- GDPR Article 30 compliance
- Dual legal basis assessment (Art. 6 + Art. 9)
- Special category data handling (Art. 9 GDPR)
- DPIA scoping (Art. 35) — triggers and outcomes
- DPA vs DSA distinction
- Spanish healthcare regulatory framework
- Third country transfer mechanisms (SCCs, DPF)

## Regulatory Framework
| Regulation | Scope |
|---|---|
| GDPR (EU) 2016/679 | Core data protection framework |
| LOPDGDD (Spain) | National implementation |
| Ley 41/2002 | Patient autonomy and clinical records |
| Real Decreto 2210/1995 | Notifiable diseases reporting |
| Ley 31/1995 | Occupational health and safety |
